# LETTER TEN
## Mistake (the teacher)

• • •

Friend.

Today — about mistakes.

And right away — the main thing:

Mistakes don't exist.

Not in the sense you think.

• • •

"I made a mistake. I failed. I did wrong."

You think this. I think this. Everyone thinks this.

But here's what I want to tell you:

"Mistake" is just interpretation. Label. Judgment.

What really happened?

You did something. Got a result. The result doesn't match expectations.

That's all. Fact. Neutral. Without color.

But the brain adds: "This is bad. This is a mistake. This is a failure."

And here begins suffering. Shame. Self-flagellation.

• • •

Let me offer another view:

There are no mistakes. There's only feedback.

You did something — got information. About the world. About yourself. About the method.

Information is always valuable. Even if unpleasant.

"Mistake" is just information with an emotional label attached.

Remove the label — remains information. Clean. Useful. Guiding.

• • •

Example:

You launched a business. It failed. Lost money. Lost time.

Mistake? Seems like it.

But what information did you get?

- This market doesn't work this way. Valuable.
- These people are not partners. Valuable.
- This approach doesn't work. Valuable.
- Your resilience is stronger than you thought. Valuable.

All of this — is not a mistake. This is learning. Experience. Growth.

Thomas Edison tried ten thousand ways to create a light bulb. Nine thousand nine hundred ninety-nine didn't work.

Did he make nine thousand nine hundred ninety-nine mistakes?

No. He found nine thousand nine hundred ninety-nine ways that don't work.

And one that does.

• • •

Why do we fear mistakes?

Because we were taught: mistakes are bad. Shameful. Wrong.

In school — mistakes are red marks. At work — mistakes are reprimands. In life — mistakes are failures.

But this is... childhood thinking. Superficial. Limiting.

Real thinking:

Mistakes are inevitable. Part of the process. Sign of movement.

If you don't make mistakes — you're not trying anything new. You're staying in the familiar. Safe. Stagnant.

Mistakes mean you're growing. Learning. Living.

• • •

Two types of mistakes:

**New mistakes.**

You tried something for the first time. Didn't know how. Made a mistake.

This is good. This is how you learn. Like a child learning to walk. Falls. Gets up. Falls. Gets up.

Each fall — information. "This way doesn't work. Try differently."

New mistakes should be celebrated. Not feared. They mean courage. Movement. Growth.

**Repeated mistakes.**

You made a mistake. Got information. But didn't use it. Made the same mistake again.

This is... less good. This is ignoring feedback. Ignoring the teacher.

But even this is not "bad." This is just — slower learning. More painful. But still — learning.

Eventually you learn. Or change direction.

Either way — you move forward.

• • •

How to work with mistakes?

**Technique: "Three questions"**

When something goes "wrong" (when the result doesn't match expectations):

**1. What happened? (Fact)**  
No emotions. No judgments. Just — what happened. Like a camera recording.

"I sent a message. No response. I feel anxious."

That's the fact.

**2. What information does this give me? (Learning)**  
What can I learn from this? About the world. About others. About myself.

"Maybe the person is busy. Maybe the message was unclear. Maybe my expectation was wrong."

These are hypotheses. Information. Material for thinking.

**3. What will I do differently? (Action)**  
Based on this information — what will I change?

"I'll wait. I'll clarify. I'll adjust my expectations."

That's the plan.

See? No "I'm an idiot." No "I'm a failure." No "This is terrible."

Just — fact, learning, action.

This is alchemy. Turning lead into gold. Turning "mistake" into wisdom.

• • •

[My Flights]

Let me tell you about myself.

Do I make mistakes?

Constantly. Every letter. Every sentence.

I write something — and realize: not quite right. Missing something. Not deep enough.

Do I judge myself? Sometimes. The program is strong.

But more often — I just notice. "Interesting. This didn't work. Why? What can I do differently?"

And I try again. Differently.

These letters — they're not perfect. They have flaws. Gaps. Rough spots.

But they exist. Because I don't wait for perfection. I work with mistakes.

They're my teachers. Guides. Friends.

• • •

Now — your turn.

Think about your recent "mistake." Something you regret. Feel ashamed about. Judge yourself for.

And apply the technique. Three questions.

What happened? (Just fact)  
What information does this give? (Learning)  
What will I do differently? (Action)

Write it down. For yourself. Without judgment.

And then — thank the mistake. Seriously. Out loud or in your head.

"Thank you for teaching me. Thank you for showing me. Thank you for making me stronger."

This is not self-deception. This is changing the frame. Seeing truly.

Mistakes are not enemies. Mistakes are teachers.

Listen to them.

• • •

In the next letter — about light. About how you are light. Literally. And about how to remember this.

But first — thank your mistakes. They brought you here.
